---
name: Research-informed parent activities
description: Evidence attribution and safety boundaries for NeuroPathways' general parent activity templates.
---

Label original general starter activities as research-informed design, not evidence-based treatment. Link to the specific studies or reviews and show their limitations; evidence for a larger program or a broad class of interventions does not establish that an individual NeuroPathways template is effective for every child. Do not reproduce proprietary curriculum materials or imply equivalence to a named program.

Context-sensitive activities require clinician review. Activities that require an authorized child-specific plan must not appear as generic parent starter suggestions and require explicit confirmation before clinician publication. Preserve child choice, communication access, and the ability to pause or stop.

For child-specific matching, a goal may refine a candidate only after explicit child preference, communication, routine, or approved strategy evidence establishes the fit. Never create an activity from a goal alone; cite the current, verified source records behind every match. For reassurance-seeking goals, do not recommend exposure or withholding reassurance.

Candidates matched to unapproved source-document behaviors, support concerns, or strategies must remain in the clinician review queue; do not publish them as parent starter ideas. Keep exact source evidence attached, and only publish after the clinician approves and assigns the candidate.

Clinician-only suggestions remain private until explicitly published; approval and publication are separate actions. A “Request More Detail” action records an internal clinician follow-up note only and must not message the family. Family-facing assignment responses must omit review notes, reviewer identity, and source excerpts.

**Why:** The research set evaluates structured programs and heterogeneous interventions, not these short original templates. Generalizing population-level evidence or a broad goal into a child's preference, safety plan, or intervention would overstate evidence and could produce an inappropriate activity. Separating review from publication also prevents draft clinical content or internal notes from reaching families prematurely.

**How to apply:** Use deterministic matches from current, verified, child-specific sources; show exact citations and keep suggestions unassigned until clinician review. Keep source links, caveats, prerequisites, exclusions, risk tier, and publication gates attached to templates. Do not recommend plan-required activities from a selected behavior alone. A general low-risk practice template matched to an unapproved document concern or strategy still requires clinician review and must not appear as a parent starter suggestion. Keep clinician approval and publication as separate authorized transitions; sanitize all family-facing projections and never connect detail-request notes to messaging.
