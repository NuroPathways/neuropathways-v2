NeuroPathways · Phase 4

Parent Activity Library

Research notes for a curated set of original, low-pressure activity templates.

Important limitation

The library is research-informed design, not evidence-based treatment. The cited studies evaluate larger structured programs or varied interventions; they do not test these individual NeuroPathways templates. The templates do not reproduce proprietary curricula or claim equivalence to RUBI, Stepping Stones Triple P, Project ImPACT, More Than Words, or TEACCH.

What the sources suggest

Communication and play. Everyday routines and shared play can be activity contexts, but outcomes are not uniform. Project ImPACT's early study involved eight parent-child dyads [1]. The More Than Words randomized trial found no significant overall child-outcome effect; subgroup findings need replication [2]. A play scoping review mapped diverse approaches without appraising study quality or establishing one effective method [3].

Visual supports and transitions. Home/community research is limited and heterogeneous, with accessibility, individual fit, and participation emphasized [4]. A TEACCH-based meta-analysis reported pooled adaptive-skill findings across varied studies but noted methodological limits [5]. A two-participant study found visual schedules alone did not reduce the behavior studied [6].

Parent coaching, communication, and assent. RUBI and Stepping Stones Triple P evaluated structured programs, not isolated generic activities; RUBI parent-rated outcomes did not reach prespecified clinical-importance thresholds, and the Stepping Stones trial was small [7] [8]. Parent-implemented FCT reviews note fidelity and maintenance gaps [9]. An assent review supports choice and attention to withdrawal signals as ethical practice, not as a controlled efficacy finding [10].

Library safeguards

  • Choose only when the child's context, prerequisites, and familiar supports fit; do not infer missing information.
  • Accept AAC, gesture, movement, speech, refusal, pauses, and stopping. Never require eye contact, speech, imitation, sitting, or continued participation.
  • Do not withhold communication devices, breaks, or preferred items to prompt a response. Do not introduce exposure, restraint, or unapproved coping techniques.
  • Context-sensitive activities require clinician review. An activity requiring an authorized child-specific plan is excluded from generic parent suggestions and requires confirmation before clinician publication.
  • Use the existing “Preview as Parent” interaction to review family-facing instructions before publishing.
Evidence gaps

No cited publication tested these templates or establishes that any template fits a particular child. AAC-specific outcomes, family and cultural variation, and direct assent/withdrawal outcomes were not comprehensively evaluated in this source set.
