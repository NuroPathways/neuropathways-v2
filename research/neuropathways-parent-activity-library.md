# NeuroPathways Parent Activity Library — Research Notes

## Summary

Phase 4 adds original, low-pressure activity templates to the existing clinician activity library. The templates are organized around communication, transitions, daily living, social play, coping, and parent preparation. Each library entry records its intended context, prerequisites, materials, duration, concrete steps, optional caregiver language, stop conditions, risk tier, exclusions, research references, and personalization fields.

The library is **research-informed design, not evidence-based treatment**. The cited studies evaluate larger, structured programs or varied interventions; they do not test the individual NeuroPathways templates. Templates are original and do not reproduce RUBI, Stepping Stones Triple P, Project ImPACT, More Than Words, or TEACCH manuals or materials.

## Findings Applied to the Product

### Communication and play

Everyday routines and shared play are reasonable contexts for interaction, but outcomes are not uniform. Project ImPACT's early multiple-baseline study involved eight parent-child dyads [1]. The More Than Words randomized trial found no significant overall child-outcome effect and reported subgroup results that require replication [2]. A scoping review mapped a broad, heterogeneous play-intervention literature without appraising study quality or establishing a single effective approach [3].

**Product decision:** templates accept familiar communication in any form, including AAC, gesture, movement, and speech; keep communication tools available; offer child-led play; never require eye contact, speech, imitation, or continued participation.

### Visual supports and transitions

The home/community visual-support review included 34 studies and found substantial variation; it emphasizes accessibility, individualization, and participation [4]. A TEACCH-based meta-analysis reported pooled adaptive-skill findings across varied settings but noted methodological and reporting limitations [5]. A transition study with two participants found that visual schedules alone did not reduce the behavior studied [6].

**Product decision:** visuals and timers are optional, familiar supports—not promises of a smoother transition and not tools for forcing participation. A template is excluded when its cue is unfamiliar, distressing, or inconsistent with the child's current plan.

### Parent coaching, communication training, and assent

The RUBI trial evaluated structured parent training with enrolled children who had disruptive behavior; parent-rated outcome differences did not meet the prespecified minimal clinically important difference [7]. The Stepping Stones Triple P trial involved 59 families and evaluated a structured program, not isolated generic activities [8]. A systematic review of parent-implemented functional communication training noted fidelity, maintenance, and access gaps [9]. An assent-focused practice review recommends attending to choice and withdrawal signals, but is not an efficacy or safety trial [10].

**Product decision:** starter activities do not teach a functional communication protocol or replace clinician direction. Instructions preserve choice, pauses, and stopping. Safety-sensitive items require explicit clinician review; an item that needs an authorized child-specific plan cannot be published without confirmation, and it is not offered as a generic parent starter suggestion.

## Library Selection Rules

1. Assign only when the listed context and required information fit the child and the current situation.
2. If a prerequisite is unknown, do not infer it or improvise. Use another suitable activity or obtain the missing information.
3. Respect familiar communication, AAC access, sensory needs, refusal, and the option to stop. Participation is never required.
4. Do not use an activity as a compliance test, create distress, withhold a preferred item or communication device, or introduce exposure, restraint, or an unapproved coping technique.
5. For context-sensitive activities, review the child's current support/safety plan and confirm suitability. For plan-required activities, the authorized current child-specific plan must explicitly support the activity.
6. Research links describe design context and limitations; they are not proof that the individual activity is effective.

Clinicians can inspect full template details in the existing assignment form and use **Preview as Parent** to see the family-facing instructions before publishing. Parent examples remain available through the current Practice experience and assigned-activity preview. No child-specific document matching or ranking was introduced in this phase.

## Evidence Limits

- No cited publication tested the NeuroPathways activity templates themselves.
- The evidence does not establish that any particular activity is appropriate for a particular child.
- Several findings are small-sample, subgroup, or heterogeneous results; their limitations are shown next to references in the library.
- AAC-specific activity outcomes, cultural and family variation, and direct assent/withdrawal outcomes were not comprehensively evaluated by this source set.
- Program findings must not be used to claim that NeuroPathways delivers or is equivalent to any named proprietary curriculum.
