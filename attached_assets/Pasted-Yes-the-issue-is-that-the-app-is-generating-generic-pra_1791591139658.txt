# Replit Prompt: Replace Generic Home Activities With Behavior-Specific Parent Activities

The current NeuroPathways home screen is generating activities that are too generic.

Right now, the app repeats phrases like:

- “Try one small, low-pressure practice opportunity”
- “Use supports already familiar”
- “Do not create a difficult situation”
- “Practice this today”
- “No aversive behaviors”

This is too vague and not useful enough for parents.

I want the home screen to show **real parent-child activities** that match the selected behavior, goal, and support plan.

Do not remove current features.

Do not change how the app works.

Do not remove safety language.

But improve the activity-generation logic and wording so parents see practical things they can actually do today.

---

## 1. Main Problem to Fix

The app should not generate the same generic activity card for every behavior.

Each behavior needs different activity suggestions.

For example:

If the behavior is **Elopement / Running Away**, the app should suggest activities that naturally practice staying near a caregiver, stopping at a boundary, following a safety cue, or remaining in one area.

Good examples:

- watch a short movie together and practice staying in the same room
- play a board game at the table
- do a “stop and come back” game in the living room
- practice waiting by the door before going outside
- play red light / green light in a safe space
- do a short puzzle together at the table
- practice walking from one room to another while staying beside caregiver

Bad generic example:

**Try one small, low-pressure practice opportunity for Elopement.**

That is not useful enough.

---

## 2. Home Activities Should Answer This Question

Each activity should answer:

**What can I actually do with my child today that practices this skill safely?**

The parent should not have to figure it out.

The app should suggest the activity.

---

## 3. Activity Card Format

Update home activity cards to use this structure:

### Activity Title

Clear, parent-friendly title.

Example:

**Movie Stay-Together Practice**

### Behavior

Show related behavior.

Example:

Elopement / Running Away

### Time

Example:

5–10 min

### What to do

Give simple instructions.

Example:

**Watch the first 5 minutes of a movie together. Ask John to stay in the room with you. Praise him for staying close.**

### What skill this practices

Example:

**Practices staying near caregiver and remaining in one safe area.**

### If it gets hard

Example:

**Shorten the time, move closer, or switch to a calmer activity.**

### Source badge

Examples:

- Starter support
- Based on uploaded plan · not clinician-reviewed
- Clinician reviewed

### Buttons

- Try Today
- Done
- We Struggled
- Show Details

Do not make the card long by default.

Use “Show Details” for extra explanation.

---

## 4. Remove Bad Generic Wording

Remove or reduce repeated generic text like:

- “Try one small, low-pressure practice opportunity for [behavior].”
- “Use supports already familiar to [child].”
- “Do not create a difficult situation just to practice.”
- “A skill from the selected support area.”
- “Practice this today · [behavior] · 5 min”
- “No aversive behaviors”

Replace with real activity names and instructions.

Safety wording can still appear, but it should be short and not repeated on every card.

Use one general note at the top:

**Only try activities that feel safe and realistic today. Stop if the activity becomes stressful or unsafe.**

Do not show goals like:

**No aversive behaviors**

That sounds clinical, vague, and awkward.

Replace with parent-friendly wording.

Examples:

Instead of:

**No aversive behaviors**

Use:

**Use safe hands and safe objects**

or:

**Keep items safe during frustration**

or:

**Use a calm replacement action**

Instead of:

**Reduce maladaptive behavior**

Use:

**Use a safer way to ask for help**

Instead of:

**Increase compliance**

Use:

Instead of:

**Extinction procedure**

Use only clinician-approved wording and do not simplify into unsafe instructions.

Create a behavior-specific activity library.

Activities should be tied to behavior type, goal, skill, and support strategy.

Each behavior should have multiple activity options.

# Elopement / Running Away Activities

Purpose:

Practice staying near caregiver, stopping at boundaries, responding to safety cues, and remaining in one safe area.

Suggested activities:

## Movie Stay-Together Practice

**What to do:** Watch 5–10 minutes of a movie or show together. Ask the child to stay in the same room or same area. Praise staying close.

**Practices:** staying near caregiver, remaining in one area.

## Board Game at the Table

**What to do:** Play a short board game, matching game, or card game at the table. Praise the child for staying seated or staying nearby.

**Practices:** staying in one safe location.

## Red Light / Green Light

**What to do:** In a safe room or backyard, practice moving on “green light” and stopping on “red light.”

**Practices:** stopping when given a safety cue.

## Doorway Wait Practice

**What to do:** Before going outside, practice waiting by the door for 5–10 seconds until caregiver says “go.”

**Practices:** waiting before leaving, boundary safety.

## Stay-With-Me Walk

**What to do:** Walk from one room to another while the child stays beside caregiver. Praise staying close.

**Practices:** walking safely with caregiver.

## Puzzle Together

**What to do:** Complete a small puzzle together at the table or floor. Praise staying in the activity area.

**Practices:** staying in one space.

# Task Refusal Activities

Purpose:

Practice starting small, asking for help, asking for a break, using first-then, and returning to task.

Suggested activities:

## One-Step Start

**What to do:** Pick one tiny part of a task, like writing one word, putting away one toy, or answering one question. Praise the first attempt.

**Practices:** starting a task without overwhelm.

## First-Then Practice

**What to do:** Use a simple phrase: “First one step, then break.” Keep the first step very small.

**Practices:** first-then routine and task initiation.

## Choice Practice

**What to do:** Offer two acceptable choices: “Do you want to start at the table or on the floor?”

**Practices:** choosing how to start.

## Break Request Practice

**What to do:** Before a task starts, model “break please” or the child’s break signal. Let the child practice once.

**Practices:** asking for a break instead of refusing.

## Timer Challenge

**What to do:** Set a 2-minute timer and ask the child to work until the timer ends. Praise effort, not perfection.

**Practices:** tolerating short work periods.

---

# Meltdowns / Emotional Dysregulation Activities

Purpose:

Practice calming skills before escalation, identifying feelings, using calm space, and recovering safely.

Suggested activities:

## Calm Space Tour

**What to do:** When calm, walk to the calm space together and name one thing the child can use there.

**Practices:** knowing where to go when overwhelmed.

## Breathing Buddy

**What to do:** Place a stuffed animal on the child’s belly or chest and practice slow breaths for 30 seconds.

**Practices:** calming body.

## Feelings Match

**What to do:** Look at simple feeling faces or emotion cards and choose “happy,” “mad,” “tired,” or “frustrated.”

**Practices:** identifying feelings.

## Calm Choice Practice

**What to do:** Offer two calming choices: “Do you want pillow squeeze or quiet corner?”

**Practices:** choosing a calming strategy.

## Recovery Routine

**What to do:** Practice the after-meltdown routine when calm: drink water, sit quietly, choose one next step.

**Practices:** recovering safely.

---

# Aggression Activities

Purpose:

Practice safe hands, asking for space, requesting help, using calm body, and moving away safely.

Suggested activities:

## Safe Hands Game

**What to do:** Practice “safe hands” during a preferred activity. Praise gentle hands and calm body.

**Practices:** safe physical behavior.

## Ask for Space Practice

**What to do:** Model “space please” or a gesture for space. Let the child practice using it once.

**Practices:** asking for space instead of pushing/hitting.

## Help Signal Practice

**What to do:** Practice saying, pointing, or showing “help” during an easy task.

**Practices:** asking for help before frustration builds.

## Move Away Practice

**What to do:** Practice taking two steps back or moving to a calm space when frustrated.

**Practices:** safe distance and self-regulation.

## Gentle Object Practice

**What to do:** Use a soft toy or pillow and practice gentle touch.

**Practices:** body control and safe hands.

---

# Self-Injury Activities

Purpose:

Practice safer replacement actions, help requests, calming options, and early support.

Suggested activities:

## Safe Replacement Practice

**What to do:** Practice squeezing a pillow, holding a sensory item, or pushing hands together when calm.

**Practices:** replacing unsafe action with safer input.

## Help Request Practice

**What to do:** Model “help please” or use the child’s communication method. Let the child practice once.

**Practices:** asking for help before escalation.

## Sensory Choice Practice

**What to do:** Offer two safe sensory options, like squeeze ball or weighted blanket if already used.

**Practices:** choosing safe sensory support.

## Calm Body Practice

**What to do:** Practice hands down, slow breathing, or sitting in calm space for 30 seconds.

**Practices:** calming before unsafe behavior.

Important:

Do not suggest physically blocking, restraint, or crisis steps unless uploaded clinician plan includes them.

If self-injury is severe or unsafe, show safety guidance and emergency language.

---

# Transition Difficulty Activities

Purpose:

Practice ending preferred activities, using warnings, timers, visual schedules, and moving to the next activity.

Suggested activities:

## Timer Transition Practice

**What to do:** Set a timer for 2 minutes before switching from one easy activity to another.

**Practices:** responding to transition warning.

## First-Then Board

**What to do:** Show “First clean up, then snack” or another simple first-then routine.

**Practices:** understanding what comes next.

## Transition Object

**What to do:** Let the child carry a small item from one activity to the next.

**Practices:** moving between activities.

## One More Minute Practice

**What to do:** Practice asking for “one more minute,” then transition when the timer ends.

**Practices:** appropriate transition request.

## Walk Together

**What to do:** Walk with the child from one area to another and praise movement toward the next activity.

**Practices:** following transition steps.

# Property Destruction Activities

Purpose:

Practice safe object use, asking for help, taking a break, and using replacement actions.

Suggested activities:

## Safe Object Practice

**What to do:** Practice using a soft object, stress ball, or pillow when frustrated.

**Practices:** using safe items instead of throwing/breaking.

## Put It Down Practice

**What to do:** During a calm moment, practice picking up an item and putting it down gently.

**Practices:** safe handling.

## Ask for Help Before Throwing

**What to do:** Model “help please” when a toy or task is frustrating.

**Practices:** asking for help before escalation.

## Clean-Up Reset

**What to do:** Practice putting one item back in its place after play.

**Practices:** repairing and resetting environment.

## Break Instead of Throw

**What to do:** Practice walking to a break spot when frustrated with an object.

**Practices:** replacing throwing/breaking with a break.

Do not use the phrase:

**No aversive behaviors**

Use parent-friendly language instead.

Purpose:

Practice routines one small step at a time.

Suggested activities:

**Practices:** task analysis and independence.

**What to do:** Practice one step of putting on shoes, such as opening the shoe or placing foot inside.

**Practices:** daily routine participation.

**Practices:** hygiene routine.

## Picture Routine Practice

**What to do:** Use a picture, gesture, or simple cue for one routine step.

**Practices:** following routine support.

## Clean-Up One Item

**What to do:** Put away one toy, one dish, or one clothing item.

**Practices:** completing one small routine step.

## 8. Use Preferred Items and Natural Activities

When the plan includes preferred items or reinforcers, use them to create normal activities.

Examples:

If child likes train videos:

**Train Video Waiting Practice**

Watch a short train video together. Pause after 30 seconds and practice asking for “more” or waiting for 5 seconds.

If child likes trampoline:

**Trampoline Turn Practice**

Practice waiting for “go,” jumping for a short turn, then stopping when the timer ends.

If child likes iPad:

**iPad First-Then Practice**

First complete one small step, then use the iPad for the planned short break.

If child likes puzzles:

**Puzzle Stay-Together Practice**

Complete a small puzzle together while practicing staying seated or staying nearby.

Activities should feel like normal parent-child moments, not therapy homework.
