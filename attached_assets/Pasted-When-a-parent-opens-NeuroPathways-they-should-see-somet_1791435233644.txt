When a parent opens NeuroPathways, they should see something like:
Support for John
Then immediately underneath:
I Need Help Now
And below that, instead of charts and document summaries:
Today for John
- Practice requesting a break during homework
- 5-minute transition activity before dinner
- Reinforce independent dressing twice today
- Try the calming routine before bedtime
- Complete one preferred/non-preferred task sequence
These activities should not be generic recommendations. They should be generated from the child's treatment plan, BIP, goals, clinician notes, and reinforcement strategies.
That gives the parent a reason to open the app on a normal Tuesday—not only when their child is having a meltdown.
2. Turn treatment goals into actual activities
Right now a goal like:
“John will appropriately request a break in 80% of opportunities.”

means almost nothing to a parent.
NeuroPathways should convert that into:
Practice this today
During homework, give John one opportunity to ask for a break before prompting him. If he asks appropriately, provide the break and praise him immediately.

Then:
Why you're doing this
Supports John's goal: Requesting breaks independently.
Done
✓ Completed
○ Didn't get a chance
○ We struggled with this
Now your product is translating the clinical plan into behavioral implementation, not merely simplifying language.
3. Keep “I Need Help Now” as the crisis/use-in-the-moment feature
This remains one of your strongest features.
Parent taps:
I Need Help Now → Homework Refusal
Then show only a few blocks:
What may be happening
John often refuses homework when tasks feel too long.
Do this now
1. Reduce the task to one small step.
2. Offer the break-request strategy from his plan.
3. Reinforce completion of the first step immediately.
Avoid
- Repeating the instruction rapidly
- Removing the task completely
- Introducing a new coping strategy not already taught
If this escalates
Follow John's safety plan.
Afterward
“Did this work?”
That last question is extremely important.
4. Build the feedback loop after every intervention
This is where NeuroPathways becomes far more valuable to clinicians.
After a support card:
What happened?
- Worked well
- Helped somewhat
- Didn't work
- Behavior escalated
Then maybe one optional prompt:
Anything the clinician should know?
That turns normal parent use into structured information.
Eventually the clinician sees:
Homework Refusal – Last 30 Days
- 12 incidents
- Break-request strategy used 8 times
- Successful 6/8
- Visual schedule successful 2/5
- Most incidents occurred 4–6 PM
- Parent reported difficulty when homework exceeded 20 minutes
Now you're producing something clinicians don't normally have:
structured information about treatment implementation outside the clinic.
That is much closer to your moat.
