## 4. No-plan experience

No sensitive upload is required to use approved general education after clinical review. Families may provide optional preferences without supplying a care plan. Starter topics link to shared guidance and suitable calm activities in the content model. These links appear in Practice or Menu, never inside Help Now.

An activity practices a named skill. A parent can select its related goal, then confirm or change the observable milestone, context, communication method and acceptable support. Numeric thresholds, observation windows and baseline remain blank until explicitly configured. No diagnosis label creates a goal, predicts a function, prescribes a contingency or supplies a mastery threshold.

**Example:** Calm Space Tour practices access to a preferred comfortable place or support. Its optional milestone is that the child indicates a preferred place or support using their usual communication in the chosen context. “Tried together” records participation only. It does not mean a meltdown improved, the child self-regulates independently, or the milestone has been achieved.

## 6. Universal applicability and safety rules

Accept speech, signs, gestures, familiar objects and existing AAC. Use the family's language and the child's understood symbols or photos. Keep communication tools accessible. Respect refusal and preferences. Never require eye contact, spoken repetition or physical prompting. Stop practice when declined, distress increases, pain is suspected or safety is uncertain.

High-risk labels do not unlock a generic intervention. If a current individualized safety plan is available and authorized, show its exact approved version. If absent, say clearly that individualized crisis instructions are unavailable. For immediate danger, serious injury, breathing difficulty, loss of responsiveness or a missing child, display the local emergency route prominently; the US starter wording uses 911. Localize emergency information before release.

No extinction, punishment, restraint, seclusion, forced sensory exposure, medication advice, deprivation of communication tools or newly invented reinforcement schedules. No safety-skill activity replaces supervision. No starter content is presented as individualized treatment. Qualified reviewers must evaluate both the general education and the escalation wording before release.

## 7. Review, provenance and retrieval

Every topic guidance card, activity and goal template has source IDs, applicability, exclusions, accommodations and `draft_pending_qualified_review`. Source retrieval is not clinical review. Reviewer, review date, approval scope and renewal date are null. `publishable` and `production_eligible` are false. Source labels must distinguish **general starter education**, **parent preference**, **uploaded plan awaiting confirmation** and **current clinician-approved plan**.

Review should include a qualified autism/behavior clinician, SLP for communication content and an OT where sensory treatment/equipment is contemplated. Parents and autistic advisors should assess language, choice and usability. Record reviewer identity/credentials, content version/hash, approval scope and review date. Any clinical text change invalidates its prior approval. Review sources and reuse rights before embedding third-party graphics or copying resource text.

Urgent retrieval must filter by approved status, exact content version, review validity, applicability, risk tier and child authorization **before** retrieval. Drafts, social posts and unconfirmed extracted strategies are ineligible. High-risk content requires a valid plan relationship; otherwise use an approved safety fallback. A retrieved source is never itself permission to generate a novel intervention.

Help Now should render reviewed static wording and approved deterministic branches. An AI model must not improvise urgent steps, reinterpret crisis plans, fill missing plan instructions or turn an uncertain label into a diagnosis. Contextual chat may explain eligible educational material within scope and must cite the card/source version. When no eligible content exists, show a clear unavailable/seek-care response. Fail closed if review metadata is missing.

## 8. Goals, feedback and shared History

Each goal instance belongs to a child and records origin, parent selection, confirmed milestone, context, communication mode, allowed support and any voluntarily configured observation window/target. Parent-selected goals survive uploads, extraction updates and clinic linking. Keep parent goals distinct from clinician treatment objectives, even when they relate to the same skill.

Feedback choices in the starter activities are **Tried together**, **Declined or stopped**, and **Not today**, with an optional note. Record one shared History event for the activity instance, content version, child and event UUID. Goals and Practice reference that same event. Do not create a second goal observation from activity completion. Manual observations remain available separately and can optionally reference a goal or activity.

Store edits/undo as revisions or tombstones so charts recompute correctly and accidental duplicate taps do not count twice. Preserve original observation time separately from sync time. Report “activity participation” and “parent observations” separately. Do not label either as clinical improvement. Declining, skipping or pausing is not failure.

The catalog's eight milestone templates contain observable wording but no default frequency, percentage, timer, streak or success threshold. The parent confirms the wording before it becomes active. Changing a milestone starts a new measurement definition/version so older observations are not silently compared with a new target.

### Calm Space Tour

**ID:** `activity-calm-space-tour`  
**Status:** Draft pending qualified review.  
**Purpose:** Find a comfortable option before a stressful moment.  
**Skill:** Access a preferred comfortable place or support

**Materials:** A child-approved space, Optional familiar comfort item.

1. Invite your child to look at the space while calm.
2. Offer the familiar item, or let them decline.
3. Show how they can choose this option or leave.

**Use when:** Child is calm; space is freely accessible and safe.

### Caregiver Safety Readiness

**ID:** `activity-safety-plan-readiness`  
**Status:** Draft pending qualified review.  
**Purpose:** Check that caregivers can find the current response information.  
**Skill:** Caregiver preparedness for individualized safety support

**Materials:** Existing safety plan if present, Local emergency contact information.

1. Check which plan version and contacts are current.
2. Identify who supervises and who calls for help.
3. Ask the care team to clarify missing instructions before relying on the plan.

**Use when:** Caregiver-only preparation outside an urgent event.  
**Exclude:** Do not rehearse dangerous behavior with the child, train restraint or treat preparation as a child safety outcome.  
**Stop:** Stop if declined, distress increases, pain is suspected or safety is uncertain.

**Optional parent-selected milestone:** Caregiver can locate the current individualized safety instructions and identify the responsible adult. Confirm context, communication method and allowed support. Baseline and target remain unset.
