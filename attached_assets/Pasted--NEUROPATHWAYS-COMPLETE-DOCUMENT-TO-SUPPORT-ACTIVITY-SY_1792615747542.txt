# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

I want to fix one of the most important underlying systems in NeuroPathways: **how clinical documents become individualized support strategies, real parent-child activities, goals, and clinician assignments.**

The application's visual redesign is largely complete, and I like its current appearance.

Do not redesign the app again.

The current problem is not primarily the interface. It is that NeuroPathways is incorrectly interpreting, generating, and displaying information extracted from client documents.

In the October 21 screen recording, I noticed three major issues:

**Problem 1 — Goals are being displayed as activities.**

In Emily Carter's account, activities contain descriptions such as:

- Practice decreasing reassurance-seeking behaviors by 50%.
- Practice independently using three coping skills.
- Practice decreasing overall anxiety symptoms.
- Practice achieving a long-term goal.

These are clinical goals, not actual activities.

A parent needs something concrete they can do with their child, including materials, instructions, a clear beginning, and an ending.

**Problem 2 — Generalized behaviors are appearing automatically in Help Now.**

Emily's account includes generalized support for behaviors such as:

- Elopement
- Self-Injury
- Aggression
- Meltdowns
- Task Refusal
- Transition Difficulty

These should not automatically appear simply because generic content exists in the application's Support Library.

If an individualized strategy is not supported by an applicable authorized plan, it should not be presented as the child's treatment guidance.

A parent should be able to explicitly select appropriate general starter support when desired, but it must remain clearly labeled as general information rather than individualized clinical guidance.

**Problem 3 — Documents are not always being successfully processed and connected to the correct support features.**

The recording shows document reading failures.

I want the system to distinguish a failed document scan from a document that genuinely contains no relevant information.

It must never compensate for missing clinical information by inventing interventions or generating generic activities disguised as personalized recommendations.

## What I Want NeuroPathways to Become

The system should work like this:

**Clinical Document → Structured Extraction → Goals & Strategies → Activity Suggestions → Review & Approval → Parent Practice → Feedback → Clinician Review**

For immediate support:

**Clinical Document → Applicable Authorized Strategy → Help Now**

These are separate workflows.

A goal is not an activity.

A behavior category is not an individualized intervention.

A document upload is not automatically clinical approval.

AI may help extract, organize, simplify, and propose content.

Clinicians must retain control over individualized treatment recommendations.

Parents without clinicians must still have access to clearly labeled, appropriate general educational support.

# PHASE 1 — REBUILD DOCUMENT EXTRACTION AND CLASSIFICATION

## Objective

Make NeuroPathways correctly understand the different information types contained in clinical documents.

A treatment plan may contain goals, observations, strategies, safety information, and home-practice recommendations.

Those should not all become the same kind of card.

## Required Classification System

Extract and store information in distinct categories.

### 1. Child Profile Information

When explicitly documented, extract:

- Relevant age/developmental information
- Communication preferences
- AAC or other established communication methods
- Sensory preferences
- Interests and preferred activities
- Existing accommodations
- Known support needs
- Relevant safety considerations

Do not invent missing profile information.

### 2. Behaviors

Extract:

- Documented behavior name
- Observable description
- Relevant contexts
- Antecedents when documented
- Warning signs when documented
- Known risk factors
- Supporting document reference

Do not infer the function of behavior merely from its name.

### 3. Goals

Extract:

- Goal title
- Full clinical description
- Skill domain
- Measurement method
- Target criteria
- Milestones
- Relevant dates
- Related behaviors
- Original source

A goal should remain a goal until explicitly connected to an actual activity or intervention.

### 4. Support Strategies

Extract:

- Applicable behavior or situation
- Exact documented strategy
- Required materials
- Communication support
- Applicable context
- Preconditions
- Limitations
- Stop conditions
- Escalation rules
- Source reference

Preserve the distinction between what a document says and what a clinician has approved for publication.

### 5. Safety and Crisis Plans

Extract these separately.

Safety plans should not become routine Practice activities.

Keep emergency guidance and safety-plan access distinct from general behavior education.

### 6. Potential Home Activities

Look for:

- Explicit home-practice assignments
- Communication practice
- Visual schedules
- Task-analysis sequences
- Previously taught coping strategies
- Parent-child play opportunities
- Daily-living practice
- Clinician-modeled exercises
- Existing reinforcement or support procedures where appropriately authorized

These become **activity candidates**, not automatically published treatments.

## Provenance and Source Tracking

Each extracted object should retain:

- Child ID
- Document ID
- Document version
- Document type
- Source section or page when available
- Original supporting excerpt
- Extracted interpretation
- Extraction status
- Ambiguity indicator
- Review status
- Approval information
- Creation and update timestamps

A clinician should be able to see the exact information that supports a proposed strategy or activity.

## Conflicting Documents

Do not automatically merge contradictory instructions.

Identify:

- Superseded plans
- Expired plans
- Conflicting strategies
- Unclear approval status
- Missing instructions
- Unreadable documents

Flag these for review.

## Failed Scans

If scanning fails:

- Preserve the original document.
- Record the failure.
- Display an understandable error.
- Allow supported retries.
- Avoid duplicate extraction.
- Do not fabricate source content.
- Do not create supposedly document-based cards.

Previously generated cards tied to a source that cannot be validated should be flagged for review rather than silently deleted.

## Phase 1 Acceptance Criteria

Demonstrate that one synthetic document can produce distinct records for:

- Goal
- Behavior
- Support strategy
- Safety instruction
- Potential activity

Show the source of every extracted item.

# PHASE 2 — FIX HELP NOW CONTENT ELIGIBILITY

## Objective

Stop automatically presenting generic support as if it belongs to an individual child's plan.

This is a critical product and safety requirement.

## New Help Now Eligibility Rule

A behavior-specific Help Now card may appear only through an authorized route.

### Route A — Clinician-Approved Support

The child has a relevant, active, published, clinician-approved strategy.

The strategy must apply to the selected behavior and context.

Show the appropriate source and approval status.

### Route B — Parent-Selected General Support

A parent explicitly chooses a general support area from the Support Library and enables it for the selected child.

Label it clearly:

**General starter support — not individualized**

This is general educational information, not a clinical treatment plan.

Do not imply clinician approval.

For higher-risk behaviors, restrict the content to safe educational and emergency-access information unless applicable individualized guidance has been verified.

### Route C — Parent-Uploaded Document Information

Independent parents may upload their own documents.

Allow them to access and understand their document information.

For suitable low-risk content, provide clearly labeled document-referenced support consistent with the application's safety controls.

Do not automatically label parent-uploaded content as clinician-reviewed.

Do not automatically transform unreviewed documents into novel treatment instructions.

Clinician approval is required before presenting newly generated individualized clinical strategies as care-team-approved guidance.

## What Must NOT Trigger Help Now Content

A behavior should not appear as an individualized support card merely because:

- The behavior exists in a generic library.
- It is common among autistic children.
- The child has an autism diagnosis.
- The behavior appears in an unrelated document.
- A generic template exists.
- AI guesses that the child might experience it.
- The goal mentions a related behavior without applicable instructions.
- A failed document scan leaves missing information.

## Support Library

Keep the Support Library completely functional.

Do not delete general educational resources.

The change is that general library content should not automatically populate individualized Help Now guidance.

## What Happens When No Guidance Exists

Show an accurate, concise message:

**No individualized support steps are available for this situation yet.**

Where relevant, preserve access to:

- Uploaded documents
- Support Library
- Care-team contact
- Existing safety plan
- Emergency information

Do not invent a replacement intervention.

## High-Risk Content

For self-injury, severe aggression, elopement, medical concerns, or immediate danger:

- Require appropriate individualized guidance for treatment-specific steps.
- Do not improvise restraint, blocking, extinction, or crisis procedures.
- Preserve access to the active safety plan where one exists.
- Keep emergency assistance instructions visible.
- Do not present general activities as a substitute for a safety plan.

## Preserve Existing Visual Design

Do not change:

- Automatic Help Now opening pop-up
- Solid red Help Now button
- Colored Support Now options
- Color-coded behavior cards
- Step-by-step interaction
- Existing emergency-access shortcuts

Only correct eligibility, provenance, and authorization.

## Existing Data

Do not delete old generalized cards.

Classify them properly and stop displaying unauthorized items.

Preserve legitimate parent selections.

Provide a safe migration or reclassification process.

## Phase 2 Acceptance Criteria

Demonstrate:

2. A parent can explicitly enable eligible general support.

# PHASE 3 — REBUILD ACTIVITY GENERATION

## Objective

Make Practice contain actual activities rather than reworded goals.

## Definition of a Real Activity

Every activity must answer:

**What can the parent and child actually do together?**

A valid activity should contain:

1. Short activity title
2. Target skill
3. Related goal when applicable
4. Suggested context
5. Estimated duration
6. Materials
7. Clear starting action
8. Two to five concrete steps
9. Clear ending
10. Pause or stop conditions
11. Optional observation prompts
12. Accurate source information

## Examples of Invalid Activities

Do not publish:

"Practice reducing anxiety symptoms."

"Practice improving functional communication."

"Practice goal 4."

"Work on decreasing reassurance seeking."

"Use an opportunity to practice the goal."

These are not complete activities.

## Examples of Valid Activity Formats

### Activity: Build a Train Together

Skill: Communication and shared play

Duration: 5 minutes

Materials: Familiar train toys

Steps:

1. Invite the child to select train pieces.
2. Join the child's play.
3. Take turns adding pieces if the child is comfortable.
4. Respond to communication using the child's preferred method.
5. Finish when interest decreases or the activity naturally ends.

Why it may be useful:

Supports enjoyable shared interaction and natural communication opportunities.

Only personalize this with trains if that preference is actually documented or selected.

### Activity: What Happens Next?

Skill: Transitions

Duration: 3 minutes

Materials: Two-picture visual sequence

Steps:

1. Choose two familiar activities.
2. Show the current activity.
3. Show what happens next.
4. Allow the child time to understand or respond.
5. Finish the short sequence.

Use this as a general educational activity or appropriately approved plan-linked activity depending on source and context.

### Activity: Choose a Familiar Coping Tool

Skill: Coping and self-advocacy

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Materials: Two coping tools already known to the child

Steps:

1. Review the two familiar options while calm.
2. Invite the child to choose.
3. Practice the selected existing strategy using its actual instructions.
4. Finish and ask which option felt more comfortable.

This is only an individualized plan-based activity if the relevant tools and instructions are actually documented and authorized.

## Activity Generation Rules

Do not simply prepend "Practice:" to a goal.

Do not copy a goal sentence into the activity description.

Do not make every activity say:

"Try one low-pressure practice opportunity."

Do not generate vague steps such as:

"Work toward this goal."

Do not invent clinical procedures.

Do not use the same generic instructions for completely different behaviors.

Do not fill empty activity lists with placeholder cards.

## Activity Validation

Before publication, verify:

- The title describes an action.
- The activity has actual steps.
- A parent can understand how to begin.
- The activity has a clear finish.
- The context is appropriate.
- The source label is accurate.
- Required clinical details exist.
- Safety conditions are respected.
- The content is not simply a goal statement.

If incomplete, classify as:

**Needs Details**

or

**Needs Clinician Review**

Do not publish an unfinished activity as usable.

## Phase 3 Acceptance Criteria

Demonstrate at least five real activities and several examples rejected because they were merely goals or lacked instructions.

**STOP. Request approval for Phase 4.**
