NeuroPathways
Legal, Privacy and Compliance Readiness Review

Prepared October 7, 2026 · Development-source review · Scope: audit, public legal pages and wording only

Not a compliance certification or legal advice. This review does not establish HIPAA, COPPA, CAN-SPAM, DMCA, app-store, or state-law compliance. Significant technical and operator-readiness gaps remain. The app is not cleared for an unrestricted public launch by this review.

1. Scope and verification limits

The owner explicitly prohibited changes to infrastructure and app operations and approved only an audit plus legal pages and wording updates. The attached audit's requests for age gates, upload restrictions, deletion systems, analytics controls, and new subscription or email processes were therefore assessed, not implemented.

Reviewed web UI, mobile configuration and relevant screens, account/consent routes, document storage and access routes, AI and engagement handling, logging, dependencies named in manifests, and existing privacy wording. Verification included the web test suite, a production-mode frontend build, development HTTP headers, and unauthenticated requests to two protected document endpoints. No accounts were created, no records were changed or deleted, no emails or AI requests were sent, and no production data was accessed.

This was not a penetration test, dependency vulnerability scan, production configuration review, processor-contract review, or attorney opinion. Source-code absence findings cannot rule out scripts injected by hosting, integrations, or future releases.

2. Changes actually made

Existing operations preserved: no backend, database/schema, auth permissions, storage, infrastructure, dependencies, signup validation, document processing, AI prompts, telemetry payloads, subscription flows, or deletion behavior changed.
  • Made the existing /privacy page and new informational pages accessible independently of account-specific routing. Other paths still enter the original account router and its unchanged access rules.
  • Preserved the accepted Consumer Health Privacy Policy's text, effective date and version 1.0. Added navigation and page metadata only. New explanations are in a separate supplemental notice.
  • Added /privacy-details: feature-data notice covering accounts, sensitive records, AI/provider processing, first-party engagement, Google Fonts and current deletion limitations.
  • Added /terms, /contact, /copyright and /data-requests. Terms, Copyright and Data requests remain labeled review drafts while legal and operational procedures need review. They do not create consent, email delivery, takedown or deletion mechanisms.
  • The operator supplied NeuroPathways as its business name and NuroPathways@gmail.com as its public contact. These exact details are now displayed with email links on Terms, Contact, Copyright and Data requests. Mailbox delivery and request processing have not been independently tested.
  • Linked legal information from the public landing footer and privacy page.
  • Replaced the landing page's overly broad “notes stay inside your private account” wording with disclosure of authorized sharing and AI service-provider processing.
  • Added or clarified visible AI/emergency disclaimers, general starter-support limitations, uploaded-plan review limitations, and the optional nickname/privacy note during parent child-profile setup.
  • Added automated tests for all legal pages, unchanged privacy-policy provenance, existing-app route fallback and metadata cleanup.

Not implemented: age verification or new signup checkboxes, self-hosted fonts, analytics consent/preferences, telemetry filtering, marketing unsubscribe, subscriptions, file-security changes, new account deletion/export, DMCA operations, revised security headers, or mobile workflow changes.

3. Priority findings before launch

  1. Account-holder age and authority are not enforced. Parent and clinician registration do not validate the account holder's age. Existing parent privacy/health consent is not an under-13 signup block or verified guardian permission. A policy statement alone does not implement these safeguards.
  2. Public contact supplied; request handling still needs review. The operator-provided business name and public email are published, with instructions for privacy, deletion and copyright inquiries. Monitoring, mailbox delivery, identity verification and actual request-processing procedures remain unverified. A clinician message is not an operator privacy request.
  3. Complete account deletion and export are missing. Existing generic per-record CRUD and sign-out do not constitute deletion of files, extracted content, derived cards, AI records, links, backups and an entire account. Retention and clinic obligations remain unresolved.
  4. Legacy client AI engagement captures a chat excerpt. The client AIChat page records msg.slice(0, 80) as an engagement topic linked to the child/clinician. This can contain sensitive health text even though it is not a full conversation. Purpose, access, retention, notice and whether to remove the excerpt require review. The existing behavior was not changed.
  5. Processor and health-data obligations need confirmation. AI feature context is sent to an Anthropic-compatible service through Replit AI Integrations, and storage uses service-provider infrastructure. Relevant DPAs/BAAs, retention, training restrictions, regions and incident-notification arrangements were not verified. Do not claim that health data never leaves the app or that HIPAA compliance is established.
  6. Google Fonts still loads externally. Two web CSS imports call Google Fonts, exposing visitor connection information to the provider. Fonts were not replaced because approval was limited to pages and wording.
  7. Upload protections vary by route. Parent uploads have explicit authorization confirmation, private encrypted storage and ownership checks. Legacy, mobile and parent document paths differ in accepted formats, storage and attestations; malware scanning and uniform per-upload provenance were not verified. No new file policy was applied.

8. Operator and attorney-review decisions

  • The operator supplied NeuroPathways and its public contact email. Confirm mailbox monitoring, formal legal-entity details and business postal address where needed, jurisdictions served, and the proposed age/access model. Publishing an email is not verification of delivery or response handling.
  • Determine COPPA, state consumer-health/privacy, health-breach notification, clinic/HIPAA, consent and teen-access obligations. Confirm whether applicable processor contracts and BAAs are in place; do not infer them from encryption or hosting.
  • Review existing privacy promises against actual AI/storage processing, provider retention/training policies, data sharing and existing clinical engagement records.
  • Define and implement actionable rights-request procedures, identity verification, response timing, record/backup retention and account/document deletion responsibilities.
  • Review the draft Terms, Copyright and Data requests notices before adoption. Determine DMCA safe-harbor approach and any required agent registration and notice handling.

  • Verify published frontend headers, native privacy/support URLs, store data-safety declarations and all processors against the deployed configuration.
